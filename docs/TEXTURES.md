# Texture templates

Seven templates, one per material, all generated by
`tools/generate_textures.py` into `textures/generated/`:

| Template | Material | Objects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `skin.png` | `MAT-Skin` | `CHR-Body`, eyelids, mouth |
| `hair.png` | `MAT-Hair` | `CHR-Hair`, eyebrows |
| `eyes.png` | `MAT-Eyes` | `CHR-Eye-L`, `CHR-Eye-R` |
| `shirt.png` | `MAT-Shirt` | `CHR-Shirt` |
| `pants.png` | `MAT-Pants` | `CHR-Pants` |
| `shoes.png` | `MAT-Shoes` | `CHR-Shoes` |
| `gloves.png` | `MAT-Gloves` | `CHR-Gloves` |

```
python3 -m tools.generate_textures --size 2048 --out textures/generated
python3 -m tools.generate_textures --only skin eyes --no-guides
```

Every template is written twice. `<name>.png` is what the material loads.
`<name>_guide.png` is the same image with the **UV wireframe**, the atlas
rectangles and their labels drawn on top — open it as a reference layer,
paint on the plain one.

## Why they line up

The model is not unwrapped. Every vertex is placed by a parametrisation the
generator knows, so its UV is computed analytically as it is built: `U`
around a cross-section, `V` along the loft, mapped into a named rectangle
of `config.UV_ATLAS`. Nothing overlaps and nothing is stretched by an
automatic packer.

That also means the texture generator can ask *where on the map is this
point on the model?* — `tools/uvprobe.py` indexes the mesh and answers.
That is how the freckles land on the cheeks and the white flashes land on
the chest: they are specified as 3D positions, not as pixel coordinates, so
they stay put when the proportions change.

Seams sit down the **spine**, not down the face — `loft_indices(u_shift=…)`
rotates which column lands at U = 0.

## The atlas

`config.UV_ATLAS` is the single source of truth; each entry is
`(u0, v0, u1, v1)`.

### skin

| Region | What it covers |
| --- | --- |
| `head` | Face and skull. `U = 0.5` is the centre of the face, `U = 0`/`1` the back of the head. |
| `torso` | Neck to crotch. |
| `arm`, `leg` | Both sides share one island, so the map is symmetric. |
| `hand`, `foot` | Likewise. |
| `mouth` | The lip strip. Painted through the same almond outline the mouth mesh uses. |
| `lid_up`, `lid_lo` | Eyelid skin. `V = 1` is the free lash edge; the dark lash line lives at the top. |
| `ear` | Ear shells. |
| `face` | Spare strip. |

### eyes

`iris` holds the whole anime eye — sclera, silver iris with its darker rim
and radial striations, pupil, two specular highlights and the lash line —
painted inside the same lens outline (`surface.lens`) the eye mesh maps
into the rectangle, so the drawing and the geometry agree exactly.
`sclera` and `highlight` are reference swatches for repainting by hand.

Both eyes share the island, mirrored in world space. `U = 0` is the inner
(nose-side) corner on both.

### hair

`cap` is the scalp shell: `V = 0.97` at the crown, `V = 0.05` at the lower
edge. `strand` is shared by every clump, `V = 0.03` at the root and `0.90`
at the tip — so a horizontal band in this rectangle becomes the anime
highlight ring around the head. `brow` is the eyebrow strip.

### shirt / pants / shoes / gloves

`shirt`: `body` (`U = 0.5` is the centre of the chest, `U = 0`/`1` the
spine), `sleeve`, `collar`. The white flashes are drawn by probing 3D
points on the chest and back.

`pants`: `hip`, `leg`, `pad` (the hexagonal knee plates), `strap`.

`shoes`: `upper`, `sole`, `detail`. The sole is not a separate material —
`MeshData.remap_uv_rect` re-points the faces below the sole line at the
white `sole` patch, so one material paints both.

`gloves`: `hand`, `plate` (the knuckle plates), `cuff`, `detail`.

## Repainting by hand

1. Generate once so the guides exist.
2. Open `<name>_guide.png` as a reference layer over `<name>.png`.
3. Paint, keeping inside the labelled rectangles.
4. Save over `<name>.png`. The material reloads it — no rebuild needed.

Rebuilding with `build.py` will not overwrite existing textures; pass
`--regen-textures` when you actually want them redrawn.

## No dependencies

`tools/png.py` writes PNGs with `zlib` and `struct`, and `tools/canvas.py`
is a small software rasteriser — rectangles, ellipses, polygons, rotated
bars, gradients, stipple and a 5×7 bitmap font. Neither Pillow nor numpy is
needed, so the generator runs on a bare Python install and inside Blender's
bundled interpreter alike.
